**Item 6 — why "Q8_0", and is it the best/only encoding?** The suffix is llama.cpp's legacy quantization-format code, not a quality tier. **Q8 = 8 bits per weight**; the **_0 is the bit-layout variant** — the original "type-0" 8-bit scheme from llama.cpp's early quant-format table (a simple byte-per-weight layout with per-block scale, block size 32; "_1" is the type-1 variant, an alternative 8-bit layout that can be marginally smaller via slightly different scaling internals). It is NOT the only 8-bit encoding: Q8_1 exists (rarely used), Q8_2/Q8_3 were experimental and are gone, and the K-quant system (Q6_K, Q5_K_S/K_M...) is a DIFFERENT family of encodings that also has 8-bit-class members — but at 8 bpw the K-quant ladder tops out at Q6_K; there is no "Q8_K". So at 8 bits: **Q8_0 is the de-facto standard and the only widely-shipped 8-bit GGUF encoding** — the first-party GGUF repos (Qwen's -GGUF repos, etc.) all ship Q8_0, the study's pinned converter emits Q8_0, and llama.cpp's own docs treat Q8_0 as the canonical near-lossless rung. Is it the BEST? At 8 bpw it is the best available by being the only available; and it is the right rung for this study because 8.5 bpw (Q8_0's effective size, 1.07 GiB/B) is near-lossless (the study-#1 ladder finding: quantization is free from Q5 up, so Q8_0 buys nothing in ARC but costs nothing in speed-vs-size scaling — the cleanest size∝params relation, which is why the law and the 1.07 GiB/B constant calibrate on it). Is it the only encoding? No — it is one of two surviving 8-bit layouts (Q8_1 unused in practice), and the K-quant family is the modern encoding system below 8 bits. The study's scope ruling (quant out of scope) is unaffected: Q8_0 stays the fixed rung; the naming question is closed with the note that a Q8_0 file is 8.5 effective bpw, not 8.0, which is why params × 1.07, not params × 1.0.

### Session 33, addendum 116 - the sparseness prior: what can be calculated pre-bench, and what cannot

Author question: can channel 2 (content sparseness at the p05 turn) itself be calculated, even roughly? Any data from the tokenizer? From config.json?

THE HONEST ANSWER, in three parts.

  config.json: NOTHING for channel 2 - by design. It is architecture only (dims, layers, heads, vocab, tying) and its entire contribution is already captured: exact params -> exact file size (addendum 110), exact KV (addendum 112). Nothing in it says anything about generation behavior. Case closed.

  tokenizer files: ROUGH SIGNALS exist, all readable offline with machinery already registered (tokenizer_probe.py; no new tool):
  a. PROBE SPREAD across the two registered text sets (corpus prompts vs ARC questions). A tokenizer whose w/t varies widely by text TYPE is more exposed to the model choosing word-sparse styles on some turns - the spread is a cheap risk indicator for how much w/t can swing by content.
  b. VOCAB COMPOSITION - coverage of numerals, math symbols, markdown tokens. A tokenizer that packs math/markdown densely enables word-sparse output CHEAPLY (one token per code/math chunk), which is plausibly part of why qwen's measured p05 sits at 0.36-0.45 against probes of 0.55-0.70: the option to go sparse is cheap, and the p05 turn exercises it.
  c. --dump mode already prints measured per-turn distributions next to the probe term for exactly this comparison - the instrument for grading a and b is in place.

  THE LIMIT (registered, not negotiable): sparseness is BEHAVIORAL - the model's content choice at its p05 turn. Any pre-bench estimate is a PRIOR, not a calculation. The best rough formula available today:

  anchor ~= probe - sparseness_prior

  where the prior for an UNBENCHED family starts from the probe-vs-anchor gap of benched families (~0.15-0.25 for healthy families; qwen's measured gap) - widened to a band until the family's first bench calibrates it.

THE GRADING TEST (same data already queued for addendum 115): tomorrow's three families x multiple sizes give probe-vs-measured-anchor gaps per family. If the gaps CLUSTER across families, the prior is TRANSFERABLE (one number; the predictor stays a value); if they SCATTER, sparseness is family-specific (one calibrated constant per family - the addendum-115 model); if they vary WITHIN a family across sizes, it degrades to a family band. Either way, the pre-bench predictor's worst case is: probe minus a widened prior band, and one bench pins the family constant.

### Session 33, addendum 118 - the ARC hardness reading of the log-linear law

Author confirmation: the addendum-117 law is not just descriptive - it is the study's statement of ARC hardness in numbers. A constant slope in log10(P) means every equal step up the ARC scale costs a constant MULTIPLICATIVE factor in parameters (~2.3x per 10 ARC points at slope 40). The expectations it matches: 25% is random chance (4-way multiple choice - the fit's intercept basin sits above it), 50% is not that hard (sub-1B models reach it), but every additional ARC point is exponentially harder in parameters. REGISTERED for the study narrative: (1) the law predicts the ARC of higher-parameter runs inside a benched family - the 7B/9B Q4_0 teaser cells can carry predicted ARC before benching; (2) future quant studies can reuse the form: score depends on params through log10, and a quant's effect enters as an intercept shift (quality loss), not a slope change - testable the moment a second-quant study exists; (3) the highlight for practitioners: within a lineage, ARC-per-parameters is a fixed exchange rate - buying ARC with SIZE costs exponentially in parameters, which is exactly why the bandwidth-constrained regime is a quality plateau where you pick the family intercept, not a scale race.

### Session 33, addendum 123 - the quant-vs-context trade: why smaller quants can be strictly better

### Session 33, addendum 136b - the Challenge-tier pick: FWE (frequent-words extraction, the Zeta-distribution aggregation task)
THE SELECTION LOGIC (the ARC precedent: the study runs ARC-Challenge, not ARC-Easy - pick the tier that DISCRIMINATES, not the tier everything passes): the paper's own cross-model record names the category - AGGREGATION is hit hardest of the four (the modern runs make it vivid: GPT-5-mini drops 0.96 -> 0.0 on aggregation from 4k to 128k, the worst category collapse of any; retrieval and tracing degrade, aggregation DIES). Within aggregation there are two siblings, and the choice between them is the Challenge/Easy split: CWE (common-words extraction) samples words from a UNIFORM distribution - 10 common words appear 30x each, uncommon words 3x - a 10x frequency cliff that a model can sometimes shortcut; FWE (frequent-words extraction) samples from a ZETA (Zipf) distribution - the natural language frequency law - so distractor words are ALSO mildly frequent and the ONLY way to the answer is to genuinely count occurrences across the entire context. FWE is the RULER Challenge tier, CWE the Easy tier.
THE TASK, registered in the study's shape: a long word sequence where ~30 words appear frequently (Zeta-sampled) among low-frequency distractors; the query asks for the frequent words; credit requires the model to ATTEND TO THE WHOLE CONTEXT AND COUNT - no local window suffices, no parametric prior helps (the paper's observed failure mode, registered as the prediction's signature: small models return parametric high-frequency words - "the", "a", "and" - WITHOUT counting, e.g. Mistral-7B in the paper). It is the exact structural opposite of niah: niah is one needle in noise (find it and copy), FWE is everything-is-a-candidate (count and rank). A model that copies from context passes niah and fails FWE; a model with strong parametric priors passes ARC and fails FWE. THE TASK THAT BREAKS BOTH SHORTCUTS is the ranking instrument.
THE REGISTERED CONFIG DISCIPLINE (per addendum 130/133, before any run): the exact FWE parameters (n words, Zeta exponent, common/uncommon frequency gap, sequence construction) are read from the NVIDIA/RULER source config at build time and registered BEFORE the first run - the paper's defaults are the reference, not our invention; scoring is a set-match on the frequent-word list (the paper's partial-credit-at-k is recorded, and the study's verdict form is pinned before the run: all-or-nothing per task at n=5, same as niah, with the per-word partial credit printed as the diagnostic).
