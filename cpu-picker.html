Which model should you run on your machine? This study measured nine small language-model families (0.8B to 3B parameters) doing real long-context work on a laptop-class CPU, then certified each one with a medal for quality, earned on measured runs. Bronze (🥙 style: it can work) means the model passed at least once at that context size; silver means reliable — it passed often enough that we are confident it usually works; gold means confident — it passed often enough to trust on a bad day. Every recommendation below is a model that was actually measured doing the task — not a vendor's claimed context window. Serving from a GPU instead? GPU picker.

All measured models — the runs behind the medals

Every family the study measured, at every context size, with the raw pass counts out of 21 runs and the medal each rung earned. This is the data the recommendations come from — nothing hidden.

How the recommendation is computed. The study ran each model family 21 times per context size up a doubling ladder of context sizes (4k, 8k, 16k, … 256k context size). At each context size the model had to find hidden words in a long coded text — a pass means finding them. The raw pass counts earn the medals: bronze = at least one pass out of 21 (it can work, but expect misses); silver = reliable, roughly 13 or more passes out of 21 (we are confident it usually works); gold = confident, roughly 16 or more out of 21 (trust it on a bad day). The recommended model is the deepest silver-or-better one that (a) fits your RAM (measured whole-stack cost: weights + KV cache + buffers + runtime, cold) and (b) is predicted to stay above the 5 w/s reader line at your bandwidth (speed scales linearly with memory bandwidth: measured@102.4×BW/102.4). The full runs table below shows every pass count behind every medal.

Why only three models? Nine families were measured (Qwen3.5 0.8B/2B, Jamba2-3B, Llama-3.2-1B, MiniCPM5 1B/2B, Jamba-Reasoning-3B, RWKV7-2.9B, gemma-3-1B). Six of them did not pass the retrieval test reliably at any context size — including some models with large advertised context windows. They are excluded rather than listed with misleading numbers; the full log is in the study's lab notebook. The three shown all certified at 4k/8k; deeper certifications are being measured now. Each family appears once, at its measured configuration.

Caveats: w/s figures are the median of 5 conversations at each model's reliable depth. Reliable/conservative depths are certified under the 1-of-3-words pass rule at n=21 runs (repetition gives the confidence; the threshold is stated). Certification at 32k and deeper is in progress — empty cells there mean "not yet certified", not "failed". Jamba2's 16,384 silver is certified (11/11 in the sequential controller). The bandwidth law is validated only at the 102.4 GB/s class. Minimum-RAM values are run-shaped: treat the fit boundary as soft within ~1 GiB.
