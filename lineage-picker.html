Lineage picker

The recommended Q8_0 model for your machine, by measured ARC, among the models the study measured passing protocol v3.1 (≤ 5% of turns stall a 300-wpm reader). Lab notebook study — measured on a T14s (102.4 GB/s class), live-corpus cal-50, ARC-Challenge n=1172.

How the recommendation is computed. Candidates are the seven qwen lineage members of the overnight sweep, each measured with the v3.1 instrument (stall-rate verdict: PASS iff ≤ 5% of turns stall the reader; measured stall rates 0.0–2.6%, all PASS, 50 conversations / 267 turns each) — the only models the study has graded under the current protocol. A candidate is eligible iff (a) its measured machine cost (weights + KV + buffers + runtime, MemAvailable-delta instrument) fits your RAM, and (b) its predicted w/s at your bandwidth clears the 5.0 reader line. Bandwidth scaling is the pre-registered halving form: w/s(BW) = w/smeasured@102.4 × BW/102.4 — linear because at these sizes decode is bandwidth-dominated; for the smallest members (whose compute headroom makes the true loss smaller) it is conservative. The recommendation is the highest-ARC eligible candidate.

Excluded on instrument grounds, not on measurement: Phi-4-mini Q8_0 and Llama-3.2-3B Q8_0 passed the reader wall at n=50 (0/267 wall events each) but under the v3.0-era instrument (the addendum-66 calibration pass, before protocol v3.1 existed) — they are pending a v3.1 re-bench and are therefore not in the pool. Mistral-7B has no passing Q8_0 configuration (measured wall FAIL), and Qwen3.5-9B Q8_0 is a measured wall FAIL; their selections live at Q5_K_M, out of scope here. Quant is out of scope for this study: this tool answers the Q8_0 question only.

Caveats: w/s figures are the corpus p05 for this reader (300 wpm, 0.45 s reaction) on the study corpus (50 conversations, 267 turns, temp 0) — a different workload can move them. The halving factor 0.50 is pre-registered, not yet validated by the 51.2-class replication. Machine-cost values are run-shaped: treat the fit boundary as soft within ~1 GiB.
